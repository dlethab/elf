# Editorial guidelines

For editors deciding what the archive publishes and how. The public summary of these rules is the *Historical and editorial standards* page (`/standards`); this document is the working version. The editorial board should review it and adopt it, amended as needed, before launch.

## Principles

1. **Nothing is invented.** If the evidence does not say it, the record does not say it. A blank field is correct when the information is unknown. Never fill a gap with a plausible guess, a typical date, or a likely place.
2. **Every claim has a basis.** Sources support specific claims (a birth year, an affiliation, presence at an event), not whole pages. Cite them against the field they support.
3. **Uncertainty is kept, not rounded.** "c. 1962" stays approximate; "1960s" stays a decade; "1961 to 1963" stays a range. Never convert an approximate date into an exact one.
4. **Disagreement is recorded, not resolved by silence.** When credible accounts differ, keep each with its source or witness (*Differing accounts* on the staff record page) and explain the difference. Do not pick a winner without evidence. When the disagreement is about **when** something happened, tick "This disagreement is about the date": the timeline and the event page then tell visitors that sources disagree, instead of presenting one date as settled.
5. **People come before completeness.** Living people, families and interviewees are protected first. When in doubt, restrict.
6. **The ELF is the centre, not the boundary.** Not everyone in the independence struggle belonged to the ELF. Record each person's affiliations specifically, with dates, only where documented. Do not infer membership from association, region, family or event attendance.

## Reviewing a submission

Work through the review page from top to bottom:

- **Is it in scope?** People, families, places, events, organizations, sources and material connected with the ELF and Eritrea's independence struggle, including its wider context.
- **Is it already in the archive?** Check the duplicate hints. Prefer a correction to an existing record over a new one. Different spellings or transliterations of a name are usually the same person, but only treat them as the same on evidence.
- **Is every claim supported?** Ask for sources if a significant claim has none. A contributor's own testimony or family recollection is a valid basis, and must be labelled as such (see evidence levels below).
- **Living people.** Is the person alive or possibly alive? If so, the record is published as restricted unless you have seen their consent, or the consent of someone entitled to give it. Tick the consent confirmation only after seeing the evidence; your confirmation is recorded with your name.
- **Sensitive information.** Remove home addresses, phone numbers, health details, and anything that could endanger someone, even if true. Recording locations for interviews are given as a town or region only.
- **Tone.** Neutral and factual. No honorifics or judgements that the sources do not support. Contested terms appear in quotation marks with attribution.
- **Names.** Keep the spelling the contributor used, plus the original script where given. Add alternative spellings and transliterations as alternative names; never overwrite one spelling with another.

Then **return for revision** with a clear, polite message saying exactly what is needed, **reject** with a reason (out of scope, unsupported, harmful), or **approve** and **publish**.

## Evidence levels

Each citation and each link (affiliation, participation) carries one of these. Choose the most cautious one that fits.

| Level | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| Well supported | Reliable, independent sources agree |
| Cited, not corroborated | One cited source supports it; no independent confirmation yet |
| Firsthand testimony | A participant or eyewitness reports it |
| Family recollection | Family members remember and report it |
| Oral tradition | It is passed down within a community |
| Research hypothesis | A researcher proposes it; it is not established |
| Disputed | Sources or witnesses disagree (also record the differing accounts) |
| Unverified | Supplied by a contributor and not yet assessed |

## Photographs, documents and recordings

Decide for each file:

- **Include or leave out.** Leave out duplicates, unrelated images and anything harmful.
- **Rights.** Who made it, and who holds the rights? Uploading something does not make the uploader its creator or rights holder. Only three rights statuses allow public display: *public domain*, *copyrighted with permission granted*, and *licensed for specified uses*. If you cannot establish one of these, choose *rights unknown*; the item can still be kept for staff and, if appropriate, for researchers.
- **Access.** Public, restricted (staff and researchers), or private. A photo of a restricted person stays restricted whatever its rights.
- **Caption.** Factual: who, what, where, when, as far as known. Write "unidentified" rather than guess a name.

## Oral histories

- Publish nothing beyond what the interviewee agreed to. Consent is recorded **per use**: recording, storage, publishing the recording, the transcript, a translation, quoting excerpts, research access, downloads.
- Confirm consent at publication only after seeing evidence (a signed form, a recorded verbal consent at the start of the recording, or an equivalent you can describe). Describe the evidence in the note.
- Use an **embargo** when the interviewee asks, or when the recording mentions living people who could be harmed.
- Machine transcripts and translations are drafts until a person has checked them against the recording; mark them as such.
- If an interviewee withdraws consent, change it the same day. Earlier consent records are kept as withdrawn.

## Corrections

Treat a correction like a new submission: the same evidence standard applies. The review page shows exactly what would change. A correction that marks a public person as living restricts their record automatically. When a correction changes a significant claim, consider recording the previous version as a differing account rather than discarding it.

## Merging duplicates

Merge only when the evidence shows the two records are the same person, place or event, not because the names look alike. Give the evidence as the reason. The retired record's address redirects to the kept record permanently.

## Removal and restriction requests

Rights holders, subjects of records and their families can ask for material to be corrected, restricted or removed. Restrict first while you consider the request; decide within a reasonable, published period; record the decision and its reason; reply to the requester. Records are restricted or unpublished, never deleted, so the archive's history stays accountable.

## The forum and the archive

Forum posts are community conversation, not archive records. A useful forum discussion becomes archive content only when someone submits it through the normal contribution forms and an editor reviews it.

## Conflicts of interest

Do not review your own submissions (the site prevents it) or those of close family members, and step back from records about your own family where you can. Say so to the other editors.
