Accounts

To create an account the archive stores your email address, a username, a display name and a securely hashed password (the password itself is never stored). You choose how your contributions are attributed: by display name, by username, or anonymously. You never need to disclose your real name, location, political history or family relationships. Profile details you add (biography, languages, research interests) are shown on your public forum profile.

Sign-in security

The archive records the network address and browser of each sign-in session so that sessions can be reviewed and revoked, and records failed sign-in attempts to prevent password guessing. Staff accounts can use a second sign-in step (an authenticator app); its secret is stored on the server so that codes can be checked.

Living people

Records of people recorded as living are private by default. They, and their relationships and affiliations, do not appear in pages, search, the map, family trees, the sitemap or any other public output until an editor confirms consent.

Photographs and files

Photographs you upload may contain hidden data such as the camera model and the place where the photo was taken. The archive removes this data from every copy it shows. The untouched original is kept for preservation and is visible only to editors. Audio and video files are published as uploaded unless the server can remove their embedded data (see the archive's operations guide).

Oral histories

Recordings, transcripts and translations of interviews are published only with the interviewee's specific consent for each use, which is recorded with its evidence. Consent can be withdrawn by contacting the archive; withdrawal is recorded and takes effect immediately on the site.

Researcher access

Administrators may give vetted researchers access to restricted (not private) material. Each such access is logged.

Forum

Forum posts are public. Moderators can see reports and the earlier versions of edited posts. Moderation actions are logged.

Closing your account

An administrator can deactivate an account, which ends all of its sessions, and can then anonymise it: the email, username, display name and profile are replaced, while the historical record of what was contributed and reviewed is kept without your identity.

Cookies

The archive sets one essential cookie that keeps you signed in and protects forms against forgery, and one that remembers your language choice. It uses no advertising or analytics cookies.

Still to be decided

Retention periods for sign-in records, contact messages and files from declined submissions, the legal basis for processing, the data controller's identity and contact details, and data-transfer arrangements must be defined by the archive before launch.
